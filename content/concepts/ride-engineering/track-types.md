Track is what the wheels touch, and its construction sets a ride's feel, sound and maintenance life. Tubular steel, pioneered on Disneyland's Matterhorn Bobsleds in 1959, bends pipe rails onto a backbone and allows almost any shape; Bolliger & Mabillard instead attach rails to a square box spine of flat plates, sometimes filled with damping material to cut the roar.

Traditional wooden track stacks laminated boards and caps them with a flat steel strap. The structure flexes by design and the strap wears, giving the wild feel and the yearly retracking budget. Hybrids keep the look and lose the pain. Rocky Mountain Construction's Topper Track swaps the top layers for a formed steel channel, first on Silverwood's Tremors in 2010 and later carrying Outlaw Run's inversions in 2013; its I-Box track, first used on New Texas Giant in 2011, is a pair of I-beams bolted to wooden bents. Great Coasters International answered with Titan Track in 2020, and Intamin's prefabricated laminated wood, used on Balder, is factory-milled to the rail profile.

Each choice ripples into [fabrication](../design-process/fabrication.md) methods such as plasma-cut plate, into [wheels and bogies](../ride-engineering/wheels-and-bogies.md), into [wood versus steel](../ride-engineering/wood-vs-steel.md) behaviour, and into the [hybrid era](../history/rmc-hybrid-era.md) that reshaped old woodies.
