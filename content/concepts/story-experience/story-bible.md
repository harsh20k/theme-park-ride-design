A story bible is the shared document that holds everything true about a fictional world: who lives there, what happened before the guest arrived, what the rules are and what the place looks like. Television writers' rooms keep one so that fifty episodes stay consistent; themed design borrows the practice so that architects, prop makers, show writers and cast members all build the same world.

The backstory is the part of the bible guests never read but constantly sense. For Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge, Imagineers invented a whole planet, Batuu, and a trading post that fell quiet when hyperspace routes changed. Scott Trowbridge has said every blaster mark and scar on a building was planned and has a story. Those details only line up because someone wrote them down first.

A bible feeds [set dressing](../story-experience/set-dressing-and-props.md), [mood boards](../design-process/mood-boards.md), [cast role-play](../story-experience/cast-role-play.md) and [cross-discipline collaboration](../design-process/cross-discipline-collaboration.md). It is also the audit trail when a project is later [plussed](../story-experience/plussing.md) or rethemed.
