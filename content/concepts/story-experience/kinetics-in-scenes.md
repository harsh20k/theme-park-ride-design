Kinetics is the Imagineering word for movement that is in a scene for its own sake: a flag, a turning wheel, steam from a pipe, light on moving water, a swinging lantern. A still set looks like a model. A set with three or four small motions looks inhabited, even when no character is present. John Hench wrote about kinetics as part of a park's living quality, and Disneyland's Main Street uses vehicles, a train and flags to keep the street in motion all day.

Inside a dark ride the rule is the same at smaller scale. Pirates of the Caribbean fills its town with figures, but much of the life comes from flickering fire, dripping water and a rocking boat. Animal Kingdom's village streets add real animals, fountains and wind-moved fabric. Movement also steers the eye: whatever moves is what guests look at, so kinetics is a cheap form of attention control.

It is programmed through [figure programming](../show-technology/figure-programming.md) and [show action equipment](../show-technology/show-action-equipment.md), designed with [water features](../sensory-environment/water-features.md), and justified by [visual attention](../human-factors/visual-attention.md) research. The exact term's coinage is unconfirmed; the practice is not.
