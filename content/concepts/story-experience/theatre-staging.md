Disney calls public areas onstage and staff areas backstage, which tells you where the vocabulary came from. A dark-ride scene is a stage: the vehicle is a moving seat in the house, the figures are blocked so they face it, lighting hides the rig and shows the action, and anything the audience must not see sits in the wings.

Three stage ideas carry the most weight. Blocking, the director's plan for where performers stand and move, becomes figure placement and [figure programming](../show-technology/figure-programming.md) timed to the vehicle's position. Sightlines, the headache of thrust and in-the-round stages, become the study of what each seat in a vehicle can see. And the fourth wall, the imagined barrier that lets actors pretend the audience is not there, is deliberately broken by role-play cast and kept intact by figures that never look at you.

Stage illusions crossed over whole: [Pepper's ghost](../show-technology/peppers-ghost.md), an 1862 glass trick, fills the Haunted Mansion ballroom, and [scrims](../show-technology/scrims-and-transparency.md) and [theatrical lighting](../show-technology/theatrical-lighting.md) are standard kit. The whole [onstage/backstage](../masterplanning/onstage-backstage.md) model of a park is theatre at the scale of a town.
