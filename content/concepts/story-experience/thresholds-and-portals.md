A threshold is the physical moment a story changes. Human beings read doorways, arches, tunnels and bridges as "you are now somewhere else", a reflex Don Carson calls an architectural archetype: paired columns, a lintel, a gate. Themed design uses that reflex to make a film-style cut without a camera.

Disneyland's entrance is the first example. You pass under the railroad berm through a tunnel and come out in 1910 Main Street. At Galaxy's Edge in Florida, a tunnel through a berm carries you from Grand Avenue to Batuu, and the music, the paving and the sky line all change at once. Inside rides, portals keep working: Pirates of the Caribbean drops you down two waterfalls into caves, which is a portal in the vertical direction, and you surface in another century.

Thresholds carry most [land transitions](../masterplanning/land-transitions.md) and the whole [entry sequence](../masterplanning/entry-sequence.md). They pair with [audio zoning](../sensory-environment/audio-zoning.md) so the sound changes exactly where the walls do. Done well, a threshold is where [narrative transportation](../human-factors/narrative-transportation.md) begins; done badly, the two worlds bleed into each other and neither convinces.
