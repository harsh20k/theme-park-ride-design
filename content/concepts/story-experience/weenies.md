A weenie (Disney documents spell it both "weenie" and "wienie") is a big, attractive landmark placed where you want people to go. Walt Disney took the word from the hot dog he used to lead his poodle around the house: hold up the treat and the dog follows. Hold up a castle and the crowd follows. Disney's own definition, as reported by historian Jim Korkis, is an architectural visual icon that makes people gravitate naturally toward a location.

Disneyland's Sleeping Beauty Castle at the end of Main Street is the original. The Mark Twain steamboat pulled people into Frontierland, the Moonliner rocket into Tomorrowland, and at closing time the lit train station on its berm pulled everyone back toward the gate. At Animal Kingdom, the 145-foot Tree of Life does the same job.

A weenie is the park-scale form of a [central icon](../masterplanning/central-icon.md) and a [landmark node](../human-factors/landmarks-and-nodes.md). It makes [wayfinding](../human-factors/wayfinding.md) feel like curiosity rather than reading a [sign](../human-factors/signage-hierarchy.md), and it is why the [hub-and-spoke](../masterplanning/hub-and-spoke.md) plan works.
