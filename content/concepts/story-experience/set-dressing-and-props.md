Set dressing is the film and theatre term for everything placed in a scene that is not the architecture: furniture, objects, paper, dirt. Props are the objects a character would handle. In a themed space the "character" is often absent, so props have to imply them. A desk covered in letters says a professor works here; a cracked helmet on a hook says something went wrong.

Universal's Forbidden Journey queue is a masterclass. The Defence Against the Dark Arts classroom is dressed with an item from each professor in the films, from Lockhart's autobiographies to Moody's walking stick, and the chalkboard lists the day's lesson. The Indiana Jones Adventure queue goes further and makes props interactive: pushing a bamboo pole seems to drop a spiked ceiling.

Good dressing follows a hierarchy: a few hero props at eye level, supporting objects around them, then texture and wear from [faux finishes](../sensory-environment/faux-finishes-and-aging.md) and [materials](../sensory-environment/materials-and-texture.md). It turns a queue into an [interactive queue](../human-factors/interactive-queues.md) and protects [suspension of disbelief](../human-factors/suspension-of-disbelief.md) by removing anything that reads as "theme park".
