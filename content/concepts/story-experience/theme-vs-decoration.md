Decoration is what you add to a finished thing to make it look like something else: a coaster station with a few palm trees and a tiki sign. Theme is a story that was there before the building was drawn, so the structure, the path, the materials and the sound all come from the same source. Remove the decoration and the ride is just a ride. Remove the theme and there is nothing left to remove.

The test is coherence. John Hench argued that guests read every detail, and anything that contradicts the story produces a small unease even if nobody can name it. Thierry Coup describes the Wizarding World's goal as a world where nothing breaks the illusion. At Expedition Everest the mountain, the village, the vehicle and the queue all come from one premise, a travel company running tours into yeti country, which is why it reads as a place rather than a dressed coaster.

Theme supports [presence](../human-factors/presence.md); decoration tends to invite [breaking immersion](../human-factors/breaking-immersion.md). The line matters most for [themed facades](../design-process/themed-facades.md) and for [retheming and overlays](../operations-business/retheming-and-overlays.md), where a new paint job is often sold as a new story.
