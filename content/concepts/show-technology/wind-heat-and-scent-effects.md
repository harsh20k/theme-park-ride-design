Wind, heat and scent are the effects that happen at the seat. A 4D theatre adds them to a 3D film: air jets at the headrest for wind, water sprays and mist, scent, seat vibration and motion, strobes, fog, and sometimes leg ticklers. The Sensorium at Six Flags Power Plant in 1984 is regarded as the first commercial 4D film; Captain EO at Epcot in 1986 and Muppet*Vision 3D in 1991 made the format a park staple.

Flying rides take the same idea airborne. Soarin' injects scents into the air streams blowing on riders, citrus, pine, sagebrush and ocean mist in the original film and rose, grass and sea breeze in the 2016 version, so each landscape arrives with its own smell. Because [scent is tied to memory](../human-factors/scent-and-memory.md), these moments are often what guests recall years later.

The hardware is unglamorous: air lines, scent cartridges and heat lamps on a relay, all cued by the show controller. The park's [smellitzers](../sensory-environment/smellitzers.md) do the same job at walkway scale. Done well, the body stops doubting the picture, which is the whole point of [multisensory](../human-factors/multisensory-integration.md) design in [4D theatres](../ride-systems/4d-theatres.md) and [flying theatres](../ride-systems/flying-theatre.md) alike.
