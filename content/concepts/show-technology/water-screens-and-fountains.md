A lagoon is a stage with two tricks. First, fountains: nozzles with individually controlled valves and lights that make water dance to music, a craft that studios such as WET have turned into a discipline mixing physics, choreography and light. Second, mist screens: fine nozzles throw up a curtain of droplets dense enough to catch a projected image but thin enough to see through, so animated characters appear to float over open water.

Fantasmic! at Disneyland, opened 13 May 1992, projected animation onto three mist screens and surrounded them with fire and pyrotechnics. World of Color at Disney California Adventure, opened 11 June 2010, scaled the idea to more than 1,200 fountains and three mist screens that combine into a 380-foot surface. Water is a poor screen compared with cloth, so these shows need very bright projectors, dark skies and wind below a threshold, which is why they live in the [night-time](../sensory-environment/night-vs-day.md) [entertainment schedule](../operations-business/entertainment-scheduling.md).

The water itself is shared with the park's [water features](../sensory-environment/water-features.md), and a show that draws tens of thousands of people to one shoreline is a [crowd planning](../masterplanning/parade-and-show-crowds.md) problem before it is a show.
