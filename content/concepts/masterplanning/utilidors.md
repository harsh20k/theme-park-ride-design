Utilidors, short for utility corridors, are the service tunnels under Walt Disney World's Magic Kingdom. They are not really underground. Central Florida's water table sits so close to the surface that a basement would flood, so engineers built the corridors at ground level and raised the park on top, using soil dug from Seven Seas Lagoon. Guests walk on what is effectively the second floor, roughly 14 feet above the original grade.

Construction began in 1967 and the system opened with the park on 1 October 1971. The network covers about nine acres in a rough ring with a spine down the middle, colour-coded walls for wayfinding, and 29 stairways and lifts up into the lands. Inside are wardrobe, break rooms, offices, the control room for the park's animatronics, and an AVAC pneumatic system that shoots rubbish to a central point.

The point is [onstage and backstage](../masterplanning/onstage-backstage.md) purity: a Frontierland cowboy can reach his land without crossing Tomorrowland, and deliveries never touch Main Street. That removes [operational intrusion](../human-factors/operational-intrusion.md) and supports [cast role play](../story-experience/cast-role-play.md). Few parks have copied the full system because of its cost; most rely on screened [service roads](../masterplanning/service-roads.md) instead.
