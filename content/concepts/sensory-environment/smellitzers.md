A smellitzer is the nickname for a scent cannon: a box with a fan, a set of scented cartridges and a timer. The design is public. US patent 4,603,030, "Scent-Emitting Systems", filed by Robert E. McCarthy in September 1984 and granted in July 1986, describes holders for scent-bearing chips, a mechanism that moves the chosen holder in front of a propelling fan, and a programmed schedule of which scent fires and for how long. That schedule makes it a show device rather than an air freshener: it can be cued like a light.

The box hides in a wall, ceiling void or lamp post, and the plume is aimed so guests meet it a moment after the matching picture. Pirates of the Caribbean is the classic case, with a smoky musk for its cannon battle. On Main Street the same hardware pushes candy-kitchen aroma onto the pavement, which is as much [food and retail placement](../masterplanning/food-and-retail-placement.md) as atmosphere. Smellitzers are one branch of [wind, heat and scent effects](../show-technology/wind-heat-and-scent-effects.md) and are fired by the [show control system](../show-technology/show-control-systems.md) so that smell, light and sound land together. Odour clears slowly, so cues are spaced generously and [scent and memory](../human-factors/scent-and-memory.md) does the rest.
