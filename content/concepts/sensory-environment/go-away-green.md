Go-away green is the nickname for a family of dull, slightly grey greens that Disney paints on fences, loudspeaker boxes, bins, utility doors and whole show buildings. The colour sits between plants and sky with no edge the eye can catch, so a glance slides off it onto what the designers want seen. Where there is less greenery the idea extends to muted browns, greys and blues; a show building above the tree line may wear hazy sky-blue. John Hench wrote of choosing a neutral tone for a railing precisely because it "did not call attention to itself".

The trick is the mirror image of a [weenie](../story-experience/weenies.md). A weenie is bright, tall and saturated to pull people across a park; camouflage paint is dull and mid-value so nothing pulls. Both manage [visual intrusion](../human-factors/visual-intrusion.md), one by adding a magnet, the other by removing a snag. It is the paint-shop end of [onstage and backstage](../masterplanning/onstage-backstage.md) separation, and the reason the back of a ride building or a stretch of [utilities and central plant](../masterplanning/utilities-and-central-plant.md) can stand in plain view without breaking a scene. It only works once [sightlines](../story-experience/sightlines.md) are checked: a grey-green box against a white wall is still a box.
