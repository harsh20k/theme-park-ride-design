The on-ride camera sits where arousal peaks: the crest before a drop, the splash, the moment a creature lunges. It freezes exactly the face the [remembering self](../human-factors/experiencing-vs-remembering-self.md) will keep, and because residual excitement from the ride spills onto whatever comes next, the preview screen at unload is greeted with laughter. That is [excitation transfer](../human-factors/excitation-transfer.md) doing the selling.

The photo then works as a memory cue for years, which is why the photo wall, and now [wearable-linked](../operations-business/wearables-and-identity.md) automatic delivery, are a reliable line in [per-capita spending](../operations-business/per-capita-spending.md). Trigger placement is an engineering and show decision: a sensor on an [airtime hill](../ride-engineering/airtime-hills.md), a [lighting](../show-technology/theatrical-lighting.md) hit timed to the flash, and an unload route that passes the screen.

One caution comes from Linda Henkel's museum study: visitors who photographed objects remembered them worse than those who just looked, unless they zoomed in on a detail. Parks avoid the trap by doing the photographing for the guest, so the rider can stay in the moment and still get the picture.
