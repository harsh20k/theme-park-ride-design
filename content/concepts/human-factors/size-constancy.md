Size constancy is the habit that keeps a friend the same size as they walk away, although their retinal image shrinks. The brain divides image size by estimated distance. Feed it an image smaller than expected and it concludes the thing is further away, and therefore bigger.

That is forced perspective in one sentence. On a Disney-style Main Street the ground floor is near full size and the upper floors are progressively reduced, so a two-storey building reads as three or four. Castles do the same vertically: Disneyland's Sleeping Beauty Castle is widely reported at about 77 feet, yet it reads much taller because its stonework, windows and turrets shrink with height. It works because windows, doors and bricks are *familiar sizes*, rulers the brain trusts.

Designers use the cue in both directions. [Themed facades](../design-process/themed-facades.md) and a [central icon](../masterplanning/central-icon.md) scale up; an oversized door and furniture scale a guest down to a child or a mouse. [Figure fabrication](../design-process/figure-fabrication.md) faces the same issue: a figure meant to read as human-sized from twenty metres may be built larger than life. [Scale and intimacy](../story-experience/scale-and-intimacy.md) covers the emotional side of the same cues.
