Vision is not a camera. The brain takes a flat, upside-down, partly blurred image and infers a world from it, using rules of thumb learned over a lifetime: things that converge are receding, things that look small are far away, light comes from above, a thing hidden behind another is further back. The rules are usually right, which is why they are so hard to switch off. An illusion is simply a scene built to trigger a rule in the wrong place, as the two equal bars above show.

Theme parks are illusions at building scale. [Forced perspective](../story-experience/forced-perspective.md) shrinks upper storeys so a castle reads taller than it is; the sub-concept [size constancy](../human-factors/size-constancy.md) explains why. [Scenic painting](../design-process/scenic-painting.md) uses hazier, bluer colour to push a backdrop miles away, one of the [depth cues](../human-factors/depth-cues.md) catalogued by vision science. [Pepper's ghost](../show-technology/peppers-ghost.md) borrows a reflection the eye files as a solid object, and [infinity mirrors](../show-technology/mirrors-and-infinity-rooms.md) make a closet into a corridor.

The limit on every illusion is viewpoint. Most tricks hold only from where the designer stands the guest, which is why sightlines and guest flow are planned together.
