Most themed buildings are ordinary steel or concrete boxes wearing a costume. The costume is the facade: panels moulded to look like carved stone, half-timbered plaster or weathered brick, hung on a light frame in front of the real wall. The workhorse material is glass fibre reinforced concrete, GFRC or GRC. Alkali-resistant glass fibres carry the load, so panels can be thin and light, which cuts the weight the structure must hold and lets a small crew lift a whole cornice.

The workflow is sculptural. A master is carved by hand in foam or milled from a digital model on a five-axis CNC machine, a mould is pulled, and GFRC is sprayed into it; after curing, painters add colour and age. Pico Play's Malaysian shop makes rock and facades this way and ships them worldwide, arguing that off-site work means better quality control. Facades carry a lot of story: they are how [architecture becomes character](../story-experience/architecture-as-character.md), how upper storeys shrink for [forced perspective](../story-experience/forced-perspective.md), and how one land dissolves into the next at a [transition](../masterplanning/land-transitions.md). Joints and fixings must also resist [weathering](../ride-engineering/corrosion-and-weathering.md) for decades, which is why Shanghai's castle was modelled down to individual ornaments before fabrication.
