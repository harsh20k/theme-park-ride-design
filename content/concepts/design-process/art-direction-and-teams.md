An attraction is built by a few hundred people who mostly never meet. Someone has to hold the picture together. In themed entertainment that job splits in two. A creative lead and an [art director](../design-process/art-direction.md) protect what the thing looks and feels like, from first sketch to final paint on site. A [show producer](../design-process/show-producer.md) protects the budget, the schedule and the promise made to the client, and chairs the meetings where those collide.

Around them sit engineers, fabricators, media producers, ride vendors and park operations, each with their own language. The craft of [cross-discipline collaboration](../design-process/cross-discipline-collaboration.md) is making those languages meet early rather than at the punch list. Universal Creative's Hagrid's team describe creative and engineering refusing to work in silos; on VelociCoaster an art director led train design while a layout designer tuned the track. Formal [design reviews and gates](../design-process/design-reviews-and-gates.md) give leadership regular moments to say yes, no, or try again. The structure maps closely onto the industry's [creative](../careers/creative-roles.md) and [production roles](../careers/project-and-production-roles.md), which is why studying it is the fastest way to understand who to become.
