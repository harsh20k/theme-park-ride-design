Concept art is the first thing most people see of a new ride, and often the thing that gets it funded. An artist paints a view of the place at its best moment: the right time of day, the right crowd, the right light. The painting is not a plan. It is a promise about feeling, which is why Imagineering's own history books are mostly concept paintings and sketches.

Visual development moves in steps. Quick thumbnails explore many ideas; colour keys fix the palette that later becomes a [colour script](../sensory-environment/color-scripts.md) and a [lighting mood](../sensory-environment/lighting-mood.md); finished key art becomes the reference everyone measures the built result against. On VelociCoaster the team moved early into 3D animatics so the art could be ridden, not just viewed. The craft overlaps with film visual development, so a strong [portfolio](../careers/portfolio-building.md) in one often opens doors in the other. The next step, [schematic design](../design-process/schematic-design.md), is where the painting finally meets a tape measure.
