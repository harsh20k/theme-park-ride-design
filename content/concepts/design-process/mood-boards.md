A mood board is a wall, or a screen, of borrowed images: photographs, paint chips, fabric swatches, film stills, typefaces. Its job is to make everyone agree on a feeling before anyone commits to a drawing. Imagineering in a Box asks students to make one in the first lesson, because a shared board stops a dozen people from each imagining a different land.

Reference gathering is the serious version. For Expedition Everest, Imagineers travelled to Nepal to study architecture and culture, and that research shaped every carved beam and prayer flag. The board becomes the source for [colour choices](../sensory-environment/color-psychology.md) and [materials and textures](../sensory-environment/materials-and-texture.md), and it gives an art director something concrete to point at when a fabricator asks what "weathered" means. When a project adapts an existing film, the board also records which frames of the [intellectual property](../story-experience/ip-adaptation.md) the park must match exactly. Digital boards are easier to share across [disciplines](../design-process/cross-discipline-collaboration.md); physical ones are better for touching a real sample.
