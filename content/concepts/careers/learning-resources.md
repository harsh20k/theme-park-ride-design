You can learn most of the vocabulary of this field for free, and most of the judgement for the price of a few books and a lot of practice. The four cards below are arranged as a ladder.

Start with [Imagineering in a Box](../careers/imagineering-in-a-box.md), the free Khan Academy course taught by Disney Imagineers, which walks from world-building to attraction design to characters. Add [podcasts and channels](../careers/podcasts-and-channels.md): Defunctland and Yesterworld for history and why attractions closed, Art of Engineering for how systems work, and the Season Pass and Theme Park Academy podcasts for the voices of working designers.

Then buy a few books from the [reading list](../careers/reading-list.md). John Hench's Designing Disney explains the philosophy; Steve Alcorn and David Younger cover the engineering and the design craft; Marling's essays supply the outside critique. Finally, do the work: the [design challenges](../careers/design-challenges.md) card turns competition briefs into weekly exercises you can run alone or with a club.

Pair each layer with the rest of this knowledge base. A [blue sky](../design-process/blue-sky.md) exercise is more useful once you can produce readable [concept art](../design-process/concept-art.md), and both improve when you understand the [design process](../design-process/pipeline-overview.md) they feed.
