Themed entertainment hires for a T shape: real depth in one craft, plus enough breadth to talk to everyone else at the table. Industry veterans disagree about the balance. Tony Baxter, long-time Imagineering executive, is quoted telling candidates to be "very good at just one thing"; Bob Rogers of BRC says he prefers generalists. Both agree the depth has to be provable.

The breadth is surprisingly consistent across roles. When Falcon's Creative Group asked five staff what they look for, the answers were curiosity about other teams, clear verbal and written communication, empathy for why another discipline's change matters, and adaptability. These are the skills that make [cross-discipline collaboration](../design-process/cross-discipline-collaboration.md) work and keep [design intent](../design-process/design-intent.md) intact through the [engineering handoff](../design-process/engineering-handoff.md).

The four cards under this one cover the trainable parts: building a [portfolio](../careers/portfolio-building.md) that shows process, the [software toolkit](../careers/software-toolkit.md) each role uses, [storytelling for designers](../careers/storytelling-for-designers.md), and [systems thinking](../careers/systems-thinking.md), the habit of seeing how a change in one place moves everything else. Drawing underpins all four: even engineers and operators are expected to sketch an idea on a whiteboard during [art direction](../design-process/art-direction.md) reviews.
