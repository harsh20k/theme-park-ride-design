A river rapids ride is a wide concrete channel sloping gently downhill from the station to a low point, where pumps lift the water back to the top so it circulates all day. Round rafts of eight to twelve riders float freely in that current, spinning as they bounce off the walls. Fixed obstacles on the channel floor and rocks on frames stir the water into rapids; a wider pool with a wave machine, waterfalls and water cannons add the drenching ([water-ride hydraulics](../ride-engineering/water-ride-hydraulics.md)).

Intamin built the first, Thunder River at AstroWorld in Houston, in 1980, from a concept by park manager Bill Crandall; the prototype needed redesigned bumpers and narrowed sections in its first seasons. Today Intamin's catalogue version offers face-to-face seating, in-channel drops of up to 3.5 metres and a rotating-platform station so rafts never stop while loading ([load and unload design](../operations-business/load-unload-design.md)).

The appeal is social randomness. Because the raft spins, nobody controls who faces the waterfall, which makes the ride a machine for [surprise](../human-factors/surprise-and-delight.md) and for shared laughter in a group ([group dynamics](../human-factors/group-dynamics.md)). The landscape is as much the ride as the water, so [rockwork](../design-process/rockwork.md) teams sculpt the channel into canyons and jungle rivers.
