A hybrid coaster uses one material for the track and the other for the structure that holds it up. The idea is old: Arrow's 1960s mine trains put steel track on wooden trestles, and the 1927 Coney Island Cyclone has wooden track on a steel frame. But the word became common after 2011, when Six Flags Over Texas reopened the Texas Giant with Rocky Mountain Construction's **I-Box** steel track bolted onto its original wooden structure.

I-Box is a flat-topped steel rail that can be shaped into tight turns, overbanks and inversions a wooden track cannot manage, while keeping the lattice look and much of the old frame ([wood vs steel](../ride-engineering/wood-vs-steel.md)). Parks get a smoother, more intense ride and lower upkeep without demolishing a landmark, a cheap form of [retheming](../operations-business/retheming-and-overlays.md). RMC also sells Topper Track, a steel-capped wooden rail, and the Gravity Group and GCI offer their own steel sections.

The [RMC hybrid era](../history/rmc-hybrid-era.md) turned rough ageing woodies into record holders like Steel Vengeance, mostly by stacking [airtime hills](../ride-engineering/airtime-hills.md) where the old track had plain drops.
