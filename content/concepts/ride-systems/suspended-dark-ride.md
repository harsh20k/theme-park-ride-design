A suspended dark ride hangs its vehicles from a rail on the ceiling. The car travels above the sets rather than between them, so riders look down on scenes built below floor level. That changes what a designer can do: a city can be built as a tabletop miniature and read as full-size from above ([forced perspective](../story-experience/forced-perspective.md)), and because the vehicle rises and dips on the rail, the ride can "take off" from a bedroom window and swoop over rooftops.

Peter Pan's Flight at Disneyland (1955) defined the type with three-seat galleons on an overhead track; the Shanghai one can stop and vary speed, which the originals could not. Mack Rides' Arthur at Europa-Park (2014) is a suspended powered coaster used as a dark ride, and Vekoma sells a suspended family coaster with a dark-ride configuration.

Hanging everything from above has consequences. The building roof becomes the [structure](../ride-engineering/supports-and-foundations.md) carrying all ride loads, and [sightlines](../story-experience/sightlines.md) are planned from a high, moving eye. Evacuation is harder than from a floor car, so [emergency stop and evacuation](../safety-standards/emergency-stop-and-evacuation.md) planning shapes where the rail may run. Compare the floor-bound [Omnimover](../ride-systems/omnimover.md).
