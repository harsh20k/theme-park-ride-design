> **Formula** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\omega^2 r}{g}$
> where
> - $\theta$ is the angle the chain hangs out from vertical
> - $\omega$ is the ride's spin rate in radians per second
> - $r$ is the radius from the axis to the chair at that angle
> - $g$ is gravitational acceleration

A swing ride is a carousel whose seats are on chains. As the top rotates, the chairs swing outward until the pull of the chain balances the outward throw, which is the formula above; faster spin, wider angle, more [lateral g](../ride-engineering/lateral-g.md). The classic fairground **chair-o-plane** was designed by Zierer and built by Schwarzkopf in 1972, and Zierer's tilting-top Wave Swinger remains the standard model; Zamperla, Chance and Bertazzon make their own.

In the late 2000s Austrian maker Funtime put the swing at the top of a tower. The **Star Flyer** winches its ring of chairs up a mast of 60 to 140 metres and spins them there, so riders dangle over the whole park from a two-chain seat. Mondial's WindSeeker followed, after a lawsuit between the two firms. The height makes these rides landmarks, lit at night as a [weenie](../story-experience/weenies.md) visible across a city.

The experience is exposure rather than force: the forces are gentle, the feeling of height is not, which is a clean case of [fear versus fun](../human-factors/fear-vs-fun.md). The same exposure makes tower swings the first rides closed by [weather policies](../safety-standards/weather-policies.md) when wind rises. Riders pass a literal [threshold](../story-experience/thresholds-and-portals.md) as the gate drops and the ground falls away.
