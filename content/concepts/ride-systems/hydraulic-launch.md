A hydraulic launch is a giant stored-energy spring. Pumps force hydraulic oil into **accumulators**, squeezing nitrogen gas behind a piston for about 45 seconds. On launch, the pressure is released through hydraulic motors that spin a cable drum. The cable pulls a **catch car** running in a slot between the rails, the catch car hooks the train, and the whole set accelerates together for two to four seconds.

Intamin introduced the format on Xcelerator at Knott's Berry Farm in 2002, reaching 82 mph in 2.3 seconds. The appeal is nearly constant acceleration and a top speed that linear motors of the time could not match, which is what built the [strata coasters](../history/strata-coasters.md) Top Thrill Dragster and Kingda Ka and the 240 km/h Formula Rossa. Riders feel it as sustained [positive g](../ride-engineering/positive-g.md) pressing them into the seat.

The downside is complexity: cables, seals and the catch car wear, and the model became known for long outages ([downtime and availability](../operations-business/downtime-and-availability.md)). A train that fails to crest the top hat simply rolls back to the launch track, which the [anti-rollback](../ride-engineering/anti-rollback.md) design allows for. Intamin stopped selling the model after 2010 as LSM launches caught up.
