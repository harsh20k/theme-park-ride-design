The classic dark ride is a small electric car on a single rail, snaking through a black box full of jump scares and painted gags. The Pretzel Amusement Ride Company patented the form in 1928: a two-seat car with a heavy pretzel-shaped weight on its nose, picking up power from the rail and bursting through swinging doors from one tiny scene to the next. Over 1,400 were sold to carnivals and parks, and Blackpool's Ghost Train, a 1930 Pretzel import, still runs.

The craft is cheap theatre done well. Scenes are lit with ultraviolet so only fluorescent paint shows ([blacklight and UV paint](../show-technology/blacklight-and-uv-paint.md)); a trip plate under the rail fires a pop-up skeleton or a horn ([sensors and switches](../ride-engineering/sensors-and-switches.md)); mirrors and scrims stretch a 20-metre building into a journey ([Pepper's ghost](../show-technology/peppers-ghost.md)). Disneyland's 1955 Fantasyland rides, Mr. Toad's Wild Ride and Snow White's Scary Adventures, were Arrow-built versions of exactly this formula ([Disneyland 1955](../history/disneyland-1955.md)).

It survives because it works. Sally Dark Rides still builds blacklight rides for regional parks, and the format's jerky, hand-made charm is a reliable engine of [nostalgia](../human-factors/nostalgia.md).
