Pepper's Ghost is the trick behind nearly every transparent spirit in a dark ride. A large sheet of glass is set at an angle between the audience and the scene. Off to the side, or in a pit below, a figure stands in a black room. When a lamp lights that figure, its reflection appears in the glass, and because the audience can also see straight through the glass to the real set behind it, the reflected figure seems to stand inside the scene, slightly see-through. Dim the lamp and the ghost fades; brighten it and it solidifies. The illusion was patented by engineer Henry Dircks and popularised by John Henry Pepper at London's Royal Polytechnic in 1862. It is a clean example of how [illusions](../show-technology/illusions.md) depend on [light and color](../sensory-environment/light-and-color.md): the glass must stay invisible, so everything around it is kept dark while the hidden figure is lit hard. Good [misdirection](../show-technology/misdirection.md) then keeps guests from hunting for the pane.
