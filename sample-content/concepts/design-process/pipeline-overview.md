An attraction is not designed in one go; it passes through phases, and each phase answers a different question. Blue sky asks "what could this be?" with no budget attached. Concept asks "what is it?" and produces a story, a ride system choice and rough art. Schematic design turns that into measured plans, so engineers and estimators can price it. Design development fixes every detail that fabrication will need: show sets, figures, lighting, control logic. Fabrication builds the pieces in shops around the world; installation puts them in the building; test and adjust is the long, stressful stretch where the ride runs empty, then with staff, then with soft-opening guests, while timings and effects are tuned. Each phase has a gate where cost and [scope](../design-process/scope-and-value-engineering.md) are checked, which is why the [phase timeline](../design-process/phase-timeline.md) matters so much. The pipeline also defines which [roles](../careers/roles.md) lead at each stage: creatives early, engineers and producers later.
