A berm is a raised bank of earth, usually planted with dense trees, that runs around the edge of a park. From inside, it blocks every view of roads, car parks, hotels and power lines, so the only world a guest can see is the one the designers built. Disneyland's original berm was raised from soil dug out for its waterways, and the idea has been copied by almost every immersive park since. It is the quiet foundation of [spatial storytelling](../story-experience/spatial-storytelling.md): nothing it hides can break the story. Because the berm takes care of the big distractions, smaller ones can be handled with colour alone, which is where [go-away green](../sensory-environment/go-away-green.md) comes in. The berm is planned early as part of the overall [park structure](../masterplanning/park-structure.md), since it fixes the boundary and where future expansion can go. Where [forced perspective](../story-experience/forced-perspective.md) makes things seem bigger, the berm makes the outside disappear entirely.
